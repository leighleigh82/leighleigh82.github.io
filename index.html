How I Lead

The empathetic thought leader.

I believe the best outcomes come from a combination of technical fluency, business acumen, emotional intelligence, and genuine care for the people doing the work. I lead with why and with transparency, I listen actively, and I build environments where teams and results both thrive.

Most of all, I lead by empowering: bringing out the best in people and trusting them to own the decisions in their domain, because they are the experts closest to the work.

I pair that with the discipline of the craft: structured goals, clear cadence, and transparent reporting, plus a belief that technology should make things better for the people who use it. I lead with honesty and follow-through, because that is how trust is built.

Reading & learning from  Simon Sinek · Kim Scott · Robert Simons · Mel Robbins · John Doerr, and a faith that grounds how I show up for people.

Signature Work

Where the story shows.

The Turnaround

Restoring trust in a team that had lost it

I stepped into a team that had lost trust: in the company, in leadership, in what came next. I didn't fix it with a plan; I fixed it with presence.

I built a rhythm of one-on-ones tailored to each person and a standing cadence so no one was without connection. Slowly, trust came back.

Delivery health moved from yellow to green, relationships were re-established across the ecosystem, and I advocated for and promoted a high performer to Team Lead who had been overlooked for more than three years.

The Strategy

Building the strategic operating system for a portfolio

Leading up to six federal contracts, I was asked to define the portfolio's strategy, and went beyond the goals I was handed, reassessing them against ground truth from partners and stakeholders to author a full vision and strategy. I advised executive leadership to ground our growth in reality: rebuild relationships and expand selectively through partnership rather than overextend. I drew on graduate strategy-execution frameworks and co-created it with my team, asking what they needed to grow, stay aligned, and stay connected to our purpose.

The result re-anchored the portfolio to the company's North Star during a period of change, connected daily work to enterprise strategy through a dynamic OKR system, and was shared with executive leadership as a model built to scale.

The Program

Owning a $67.8M federal modernization program

For three and a half years I owned my company's largest federal contract, a CMS Medicaid and CHIP modernization program serving millions of Americans, leading six delivery teams, growing the program from ~64 to 75, owning its financials, and reporting performance monthly to executive leadership.

Delivered on every contractual obligation per the official Contract Closeout Statement, acknowledged by the CMS COR. Drove ~15% efficiency gains and ~$1M in cost savings, reduced defects through test automation, and increased release cadence from twice-yearly to every one to two months.

Growth & Origination

Turning delivery insight into new work

Growth isn't separate from delivery; it comes from it. While executing the current year, I built the pipeline of opportunities for the next, and originated a new service offering I researched, solutioned, and mocked up myself before pitching it to executive leadership.

On one federal contract, I reversed a planned staff reduction, authoring the proposal that protected 3 roles slated to be cut, then grew the account by ~10 net-new roles through new task work and secured the next option period with continued growth. At mid-year, the portfolio was tracking ahead of its revenue target.

Transformation

Modernizing commercial clients off legacy systems

Earlier in my career, I led legacy-modernization transformations for commercial title companies across multiple regions, running stakeholder discovery and gap analysis, driving adoption through change management and hands-on training, and partnering with users to drive continuous improvement.

The same throughline has run through my whole career: meet people where they are, build trust, and guide them through change to a better outcome.

Why I Lead
